A Field Guide to Garden Birds

How to use this guide

Each entry describes one common garden bird: what it looks like, where it
lives, how and where it nests, what its song sounds like and what it eats.
Read an entry before you step outside, then watch quietly from a window or a
bench.

## Wren

The wren is a tiny brown bird with a cocked tail, most often seen in low
hedges and ivy. For a nest it builds a domed ball of moss tucked into ivy or
a bank, and the male builds several for the female to choose from. Its song
is a loud trilling song far too big for its body, ending in a rattle. It
feeds mainly on spiders and small beetles. Look for it in early morning,
when the wren is busiest and least wary of people.

## Robin

The robin is a plump bird with an orange breast, most often seen in gardens
and woodland edges. For a nest it builds a cup of leaves and moss in a
sheltered hollow, often in sheds or old kettles. Its song is a wistful
warble sung through the winter. It feeds mainly on worms turned up by the
spade. Look for it in early morning, when the robin is busiest and least
wary of people.

## Blackbird

The blackbird is a glossy black thrush with a yellow bill, most often seen
in lawns and shrubberies. For a nest it builds a mud-lined cup of grass in a
dense shrub. Its song is a slow, fluting song at dusk. It feeds mainly on
worms, berries and fallen apples. Look for it in early morning, when the
blackbird is busiest and least wary of people.

## Song Thrush

The song thrush is a speckled brown thrush, most often seen in damp gardens
with cover. For a nest it builds a cup lined with smooth mud and rotten
wood. Its song is repeated phrases, each sung two or three times. It feeds
mainly on snails smashed on a favourite stone anvil. Look for it in early
morning, when the song thrush is busiest and least wary of people.

## Blue Tit

The blue tit is a small acrobatic bird with a blue cap, most often seen in
gardens with old trees. For a nest it builds a cavity or nest box filled
with moss and feathers. Its song is a thin high trill. It feeds mainly on
caterpillars, and peanuts from feeders in winter. Look for it in early
morning, when the blue tit is busiest and least wary of people.

## Great Tit

The great tit is a bold bird with a black stripe down a yellow belly, most
often seen in parks and woodland. For a nest it builds a tree hole or nest
box. Its song is a ringing teacher-teacher call. It feeds mainly on insects,
seeds and fat balls. Look for it in early morning, when the great tit is
busiest and least wary of people.

## Goldfinch

The goldfinch is a finch with a red face and gold wing bars, most often seen
in rough ground and gardens. For a nest it builds a neat cup of moss and
wool near the tip of a branch. Its song is a liquid tinkling twitter. It
feeds mainly on the seeds of thistles, teasels and dandelions, picked with a
fine bill. Look for it in early morning, when the goldfinch is busiest and
least wary of people.

## Greenfinch

The greenfinch is a stout olive-green finch, most often seen in hedges and
conifers. For a nest it builds a bulky cup in a thick bush. Its song is a
wheezing drawn-out note. It feeds mainly on sunflower hearts and large
seeds. Look for it in early morning, when the greenfinch is busiest and
least wary of people.

## Chaffinch

The chaffinch is a finch with a pink breast and white wing flashes, most
often seen in woodland and gardens. For a nest it builds a lichen-covered
cup that blends into the bark. Its song is a descending rattle with a
flourish at the end. It feeds mainly on seeds gathered from the ground. Look
for it in early morning, when the chaffinch is busiest and least wary of
people.

## House Sparrow

The house sparrow is a chirpy brown bird that lives in flocks, most often
seen in towns and farmyards. For a nest it builds an untidy bundle of straw
under roof tiles. Its song is constant cheerful chirping. It feeds mainly on
grain, crumbs and insects. Look for it in early morning, when the house
sparrow is busiest and least wary of people.

## Starling

The starling is a speckled bird with an oily sheen, most often seen in
lawns, roofs and reed beds. For a nest it builds a hole in a wall or roof.
Its song is a jumble of clicks, whistles and copied sounds. It feeds mainly
on leatherjackets probed from the lawn. Look for it in early morning, when
the starling is busiest and least wary of people.

## Dunnock

The dunnock is a quiet grey and brown bird, most often seen in hedge
bottoms. For a nest it builds a cup deep in a hedge. Its song is a hurried
thin warble. It feeds mainly on tiny insects picked from the soil. Look for
it in early morning, when the dunnock is busiest and least wary of people.

## Collared Dove

The collared dove is a pale pinkish-grey dove with a black half collar, most
often seen in suburbs. For a nest it builds a flimsy platform of twigs. Its
song is a monotonous three-note coo. It feeds mainly on seeds and grain.
Look for it in early morning, when the collared dove is busiest and least
wary of people.

## Woodpigeon

The woodpigeon is a large grey pigeon with white neck patches, most often
seen in fields and gardens. For a nest it builds a loose platform of twigs.
Its song is a soft five-note coo. It feeds mainly on leaves, buds, peas and
grain. Look for it in early morning, when the woodpigeon is busiest and
least wary of people.

## Magpie

The magpie is a long-tailed black and white crow, most often seen in
everywhere. For a nest it builds a domed nest of thorny sticks. Its song is
a harsh chattering rattle. It feeds mainly on almost anything, including
eggs. Look for it in early morning, when the magpie is busiest and least
wary of people.

## Jay

The jay is a shy pinkish crow with blue wing patches, most often seen in oak
woodland. For a nest it builds a twig nest in a tree fork. Its song is a
screech that alarms the wood. It feeds mainly on acorns buried for winter.
Look for it in early morning, when the jay is busiest and least wary of
people.

## Long-tailed Tit

The long-tailed tit is a tiny pink and black ball with a very long tail,
most often seen in hedges and woodland edges. For a nest it builds a
stretchy bag of moss, cobweb and lichen lined with hundreds of feathers. Its
song is thin see-see-see contact calls. It feeds mainly on small insects and
spiders. Look for it in early morning, when the long-tailed tit is busiest
and least wary of people.

## Nuthatch

The nuthatch is a blue-grey bird that climbs head-first down trees, most
often seen in mature woodland. For a nest it builds a tree hole whose
entrance it plasters with mud to make it smaller. Its song is a loud
whistled dwip. It feeds mainly on nuts wedged into bark and hammered open.
Look for it in early morning, when the nuthatch is busiest and least wary of
people.

## Siskin

The siskin is a small streaky yellow finch, most often seen in conifers. For
a nest it builds a cup high in a conifer. Its song is a wheezy twittering
song. It feeds mainly on seeds of alder, birch and spruce. Look for it in
early morning, when the siskin is busiest and least wary of people.

## Bullfinch

The bullfinch is a plump finch, the male rose-red, most often seen in
orchards and thickets. For a nest it builds a flimsy cup of twigs. Its song
is a soft piping call. It feeds mainly on buds of fruit trees in spring.
Look for it in early morning, when the bullfinch is busiest and least wary
of people.

## Pied Wagtail

The pied wagtail is a black and white bird that wags its tail, most often
seen in car parks, lawns and roofs. For a nest it builds a crevice in a wall
or a building. Its song is a sharp chiswick call in flight. It feeds mainly
on insects chased across open ground. Look for it in early morning, when the
pied wagtail is busiest and least wary of people.

## Swallow

The swallow is a summer visitor with long tail streamers, most often seen in
farms and open country. For a nest it builds a mud cup on a beam inside a
barn. Its song is a twittering song. It feeds mainly on flies caught on the
wing. Look for it in early morning, when the swallow is busiest and least
wary of people.

## House Martin

The house martin is a blue-black and white summer visitor, most often seen
in villages. For a nest it builds a mud cup under the eaves. Its song is a
dry chirrup. It feeds mainly on insects taken high in the air. Look for it
in early morning, when the house martin is busiest and least wary of people.

## Swift

The swift is a dark sickle-winged bird that almost never lands, most often
seen in town skies. For a nest it builds a hollow under roof tiles reached
in high-speed flight. Its song is screaming chases over the rooftops. It
feeds mainly on airborne insects. Look for it in early morning, when the
swift is busiest and least wary of people.

## Coal Tit

The coal tit is a small tit with a white nape patch, most often seen in
conifers. For a nest it builds a hole low in a bank or tree. Its song is a
fast two-note song. It feeds mainly on seeds it hides for later. Look for it
in early morning, when the coal tit is busiest and least wary of people.

## Blackcap

The blackcap is a grey warbler, the male with a black cap, most often seen
in gardens with shrubs. For a nest it builds a flimsy cup in brambles. Its
song is a rich, varied warble. It feeds mainly on insects in summer and
berries in autumn. Look for it in early morning, when the blackcap is
busiest and least wary of people.

## Chiffchaff

The chiffchaff is a small olive warbler, most often seen in woodland edges.
For a nest it builds a domed nest just above the ground. Its song is its own
name repeated: chiff-chaff. It feeds mainly on tiny insects among leaves.
Look for it in early morning, when the chiffchaff is busiest and least wary
of people.

## Goldcrest

The goldcrest is the smallest bird in Britain, with a gold crown stripe,
most often seen in conifers. For a nest it builds a hanging hammock of moss
slung under a conifer branch. Its song is a very high cyclic song. It feeds
mainly on springtails and aphids. Look for it in early morning, when the
goldcrest is busiest and least wary of people.

## Sparrowhawk

The sparrowhawk is a small hawk with barred underparts, most often seen in
gardens and woods. For a nest it builds a stick platform in a conifer. Its
song is a sharp kek-kek near the nest. It feeds mainly on small birds
ambushed at the feeder. Look for it in early morning, when the sparrowhawk
is busiest and least wary of people.

## Tawny Owl

The tawny owl is a round brown owl with dark eyes, most often seen in parks
and woodland. For a nest it builds a hole in an old tree. Its song is the
familiar too-whit too-whoo duet of a pair. It feeds mainly on mice and
voles. Look for it in early morning, when the tawny owl is busiest and least
wary of people.
