## Learning as optimization

A neural network is a function with millions of adjustable knobs — weights and biases.
Training means choosing knob settings that make the function's outputs match labeled
examples. The mismatch is measured by a loss function: cross-entropy for
classification, mean squared error for regression. Learning is nothing more than
minimizing this loss over the training set, and the workhorse minimizer is gradient
descent: compute the slope of the loss with respect to every weight, then nudge each
weight a small step downhill.

The step size is the learning rate. Too large and training diverges, the loss
oscillating or exploding; too small and training crawls. In practice nobody computes
the gradient over the full dataset at once — stochastic gradient descent estimates it
from a small random mini-batch, which is both cheaper and, thanks to its noise, better
at escaping shallow local minima. Optimizers like Adam keep running averages of past
gradients to adapt each weight's effective step size automatically.

## Backpropagation

The gradient of the loss with respect to a weight buried ten layers deep is computed by
backpropagation, which is the chain rule of calculus organized efficiently. A forward
pass runs the input through the network, caching each layer's activations. Then the
error signal travels backward: starting from the derivative of the loss at the output,
each layer multiplies the incoming gradient by its own local derivative and passes the
result to the layer below. One forward pass plus one backward pass yields the exact
gradient for every weight simultaneously — the weight update is then just
`w = w - lr * dL/dw` applied everywhere at once.

Deep stacks of multiplications make gradients unstable. If layer derivatives are mostly
smaller than one, the product shrinks exponentially on the way down — the vanishing
gradient problem, which froze early deep networks. ReLU activations, careful weight
initialization, residual (skip) connections, and batch normalization are all, at heart,
tricks to keep the backward signal alive through many layers.

## Generalization

A network big enough to memorize its training data will happily do so. The symptom of
overfitting is a training loss that keeps falling while validation loss turns and
rises. Regularization pushes back: dropout randomly silences units during training so
no single pathway becomes indispensable; weight decay penalizes large weights; data
augmentation manufactures new training views; and early stopping simply halts at the
validation minimum. The quantity that matters in the end is never training accuracy but
performance on data the model has not seen.
