Fermenting at Home

Before you begin

Fermentation is controlled spoilage: salt, sugar or a starter culture tips
the balance towards friendly microbes. Every entry below gives the
ingredient, the culture or salt, how long it takes, where to keep the jar,
and one tip learned the hard way.

## Sauerkraut

Sauerkraut starts from finely shredded cabbage, fermented with two percent
of its weight in salt. It takes three to six weeks in a cool cupboard. The
one thing to remember: keep the cabbage under its own brine so mould cannot
reach it. Keep notes on every batch so a good result can be repeated. A
small first batch costs little if it goes wrong. Use filtered water if the
tap water smells strongly of chlorine. A fuzzy coloured mould means the
batch goes on the compost.

## Kimchi

Kimchi starts from napa cabbage, radish and chilli paste, fermented with a
brine soak before the paste goes on. It takes one to two weeks in the fridge
after a day at room temperature. The one thing to remember: leave headroom
in the jar, because it fizzes. Clean jars matter more than expensive
equipment. Warm rooms speed everything up; cool rooms give deeper flavour.
Smell it daily: a clean ferment smells sour or yeasty, never rotten. Keep
notes on every batch so a good result can be repeated.

## Kombucha

Kombucha starts from sweet black or green tea, fermented with a scoby, the
rubbery culture of yeast and bacteria. It takes seven to ten days in a warm
shelf out of the sun. The one thing to remember: keep back a cup of the last
batch to start the next one. A small first batch costs little if it goes
wrong. Label each jar with the date it was started. A fuzzy coloured mould
means the batch goes on the compost. Clean jars matter more than expensive
equipment.

## Kefir

Kefir starts from whole milk, fermented with kefir grains that look like
small cauliflower florets. It takes one day in room temperature. The one
thing to remember: strain the grains out with a plastic sieve, never a metal
one. Warm rooms speed everything up; cool rooms give deeper flavour. Use
filtered water if the tap water smells strongly of chlorine. Keep notes on
every batch so a good result can be repeated. A small first batch costs
little if it goes wrong.

## Yoghurt

oven with the light on. The one thing to remember: a longer set makes a
sharper yoghurt. Label each jar with the date it was started. Smell it
daily: a clean ferment smells sour or yeasty, never rotten. Clean jars
matter more than expensive equipment. Warm rooms speed everything up; cool
rooms give deeper flavour.

## Miso

## Tempeh

Tempeh starts from dehulled cooked soybeans, fermented with tempeh spores.
It takes a day and a half in a warm incubator at about thirty degrees. The

compost.

## Ginger Beer

Ginger Beer starts from grated ginger, sugar and water, fermented with a
ginger bug fed for a few days. It takes two to three days in bottles in room

the bottles do not burst. Clean jars matter more than expensive equipment.

Fermented Hot Sauce starts from chillies and garlic, fermented with a three
