## The drivetrain

A chain wears with every kilometre. The pins and rollers grind down and the chain
effectively lengthens, which people call chain stretch. A stretched chain no longer meshes
cleanly with the cassette and chainrings and wears their teeth into hooks, so replacing a
cheap chain early saves replacing the expensive cogs later. Check it with a chain checker
tool every thousand kilometres or so: at 0.5 percent elongation on an 11- or 12-speed
drivetrain, or 0.75 percent on older ones, fit a new chain. Clean and lubricate it after
wet rides: wipe the chain, apply one drop of lubricant per roller, wait a few minutes and
wipe off the excess, which otherwise collects grit.

## Adjusting the rear derailleur

Two limit screws stop the derailleur from throwing the chain past the smallest or the
largest cog. Shift onto the smallest cog and turn the H screw until the jockey wheel sits
directly under that cog; shift onto the largest cog and set the L screw the same way. The
barrel adjuster then tunes cable tension: if the chain hesitates to climb to a larger cog,
turn the adjuster anticlockwise a quarter turn at a time; if it hesitates to drop to a
smaller one, turn it clockwise. A bent derailleur hanger defeats every adjustment and has
to be straightened with an alignment tool first.

## Brakes

Rim brake pads wear down to their grooves and must be replaced before the metal backing
touches the rim. Hydraulic disc brakes need their pads checked for thickness, at least
half a millimetre of material, and their rotors kept free of oil; a contaminated pad
squeals and loses power and is usually cheaper to replace than to clean. When a lever
feels spongy, air has entered the system and the brake needs bleeding with the fluid the
manufacturer specifies: mineral oil and DOT fluid must never be mixed.

## Tyres and punctures

Run the pressure printed on the tyre sidewall as a range, lower for wide tyres and rough
roads, and check it weekly because tubes lose air slowly. To fix a puncture, remove the
wheel, lever one bead off the rim, find the hole by inflating the tube and listening, and
always run a finger around the inside of the tyre to find the thorn or glass that caused
it before fitting the patched or new tube.

## Wheels

A wheel that wobbles side to side when spun is out of true. Tighten the spoke on the
opposite side of the wobble and loosen its neighbour by a quarter turn each, then spin and
check again; small, alternating corrections bring it back far more reliably than one big
turn on a single spoke.
